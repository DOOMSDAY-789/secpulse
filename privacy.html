SecPulse privacy policy

Last updated: 7 November 2026 (ads added). Applies to the SecPulse web app, Android app and Windows app.

The short version

SecPulse has no accounts and no analytics. We do not collect your personal data, and what you set in the app stays on your device. The Android app shows Google ads, and Google collects some data for those (see Ads below). The web app and Windows app have no ads.

What stays on your device

  • Your settings: watchlist, stack keywords, severity choice, alert topic.
  • Which items you have already seen, and (if you use the experimental checklist) which items you ticked and when.

Clearing the app's data or uninstalling it deletes all of this. We never receive it.

What the app sends, and to whom

  • Password check: your password is turned into a SHA-1 hash on your device. Only the first 5 characters of that hash are sent to Pwned Passwords (run by Have I Been Pwned). Your password and the rest of the hash never leave your device, and the password is never saved.
  • Site check: only when you press Check site, the site name you typed (for example shop.example.com, never a full link or anything you entered on that site) is sent to Mozilla's HTTP Observatory, which scans that site's public security settings. Mozilla's scan results for a site are public. Past leaks are looked up on your device in the list already downloaded from Have I Been Pwned. The app does not save what you checked.
  • Feeds: the app downloads public lists from Have I Been Pwned, the US National Vulnerability Database (NVD) and CISA (via GitHub). These are ordinary web requests, so those services see your IP address, as any website would. Your stack keywords are sent to NVD as search words.
  • Alerts (optional): only if you press a push button, or set up the PC check, a short message listing public breach and vulnerability news is sent to ntfy.sh under your topic name. Anyone who knows the topic name can read those messages.

Ads (Android app only)

The Android app shows a banner ad from Google AdMob on the feed screens. There are no ads on the password screen, and none in the web app or the Windows app.

  • What Google collects: to show and measure ads, the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects your device's advertising ID, IP address, general device and app information, and how you interact with ads. See how Google uses data from apps that use its services.
  • Your choice: in the UK, EU and other places that require it, the app asks for your consent the first time it starts. You can change your answer later under More → Ad privacy choices. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in Android's settings (Settings → Privacy → Ads).
  • SecPulse itself never sees or stores this ad data.

Children

SecPulse is not aimed at children under 13.
